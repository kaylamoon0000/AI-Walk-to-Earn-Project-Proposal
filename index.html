1. Project Overview

2. Problem Definition

Many pedestrian plazas struggle with low engagement, lack of exploration incentives, and safety concerns[cite: 35, 36, 38]. Without active use, these public spaces can feel empty, disconnected from local commerce, and unsafe[cite: 39, 40].

3. Site Understanding

Our analysis focuses on business activity and community movement[cite: 14, 17]. By identifying underused zones in the Harlem Plaza, we deploy strategic Landmark Checkpoints near local merchants. Users must physically visit these spots and scan a code to confirm their visit, directly connecting foot traffic to economic support[cite: 7, 12].

4. Design & AI Components

Activity Recognition

AI verifies walking via accelerometer and step detection to prevent cheating[cite: 65, 66, 67].

Route Verification

GPS data ensures users follow safe, designated pedestrian paths in the plaza[cite: 71, 72, 75].

Landmark Scanning

Physical code scans provide "Proof of Presence" at local business locations.

Reward Optimization

AI predicts incentives—like food coupons or loyalty points—that best motivate users[cite: 84, 85, 88].

5. Final Redesign Proposal

The "Imagine Harlem" system uses a mobile interface where pedestrians start verified walks, follow scenic routes, and scan landmarks to earn discounts and free items[cite: 133, 134, 137]. This system improves public health and plaza safety while providing businesses with increased customer flow[cite: 53, 60, 153].

6. Human Reflection on AI Use

AI Assistance: AI helped generate the motion detection logic [cite: 108], simulate the reward model [cite: 130], and identify optimal pedestrian routes based on data[cite: 76, 77].

Human Judgment: Human intervention was required to ensure landmark placement was culturally appropriate for Harlem, verify the safety of recommended paths, and manage the ethical collection of user data[cite: 201].
