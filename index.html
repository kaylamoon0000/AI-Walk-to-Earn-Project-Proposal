About the Project

Project Title: AI Walk-to-Earn Harlem: Incentivizing Pedestrian Activity in the Harlem Plaza.

Project Goal: To design a proposal for a smart pedestrian experience that encourages walking, verifies landmark visits, and connects activity in the plaza to rewards from local businesses.

Essential Question: How can artificial intelligence encourage people to walk more while supporting local businesses in a pedestrian plaza?

The Problem

Many pedestrian plazas struggle with low engagement, limited incentives to explore, safety concerns in underused spaces, and weak connections between foot traffic and nearby businesses.

This redesign responds to a real-world problem: people may pass through a plaza without interacting with it, and businesses nearby may not benefit from pedestrian movement. The proposal tries to solve that by making walking purposeful, verified, and rewarding.

Site / Context

The site analysis focuses on how people move through the Harlem plaza, where they feel comfortable stopping, how safe and accessible the routes are, and where business activity is strongest.

This proposal assumes landmarks can be placed at strategic points near businesses, seating zones, and high-visibility areas so the plaza supports both community movement and economic activity.

Design Options

Different proposal directions were considered before selecting the final redesign concept.

Option 1: Route Reward Model

Users earn incentives for completing verified walking routes. This improves movement but gives limited proof that users interacted with businesses or landmarks.

Option 2: Business Check-In Model

Users receive rewards by arriving near businesses. This supports local stores, but location alone may not confirm true participation in the plaza experience.

Option 3: Landmark QR Proposal

Users walk a suggested route, stop at a plaza landmark, and scan a QR code placed near a business. This creates stronger confirmation, more engagement, and a clearer redesign identity.

Final Proposal

The selected redesign combines AI walking verification, route guidance, and landmark QR checkpoints to create a more active and community-connected pedestrian plaza.

In the final redesign proposal, pedestrians follow safe routes through the plaza, arrive at landmarks placed near local businesses, and scan a QR code for visit confirmation. After the scan, the user can unlock coupons, discounts, points, or food rewards.

This proposal is justified because it creates stronger proof of real engagement, encourages people to explore more of the plaza, supports local businesses directly, and gives the site a more visible and interactive identity.

Reflection / Conclusion

This redesign proposal shows how AI can support a stronger pedestrian experience, but it also shows where human decision-making still matters.

What AI Helped With

  • Generating redesign ideas
  • Imagining proposal visuals
  • Organizing route and reward concepts
  • Supporting the structure of the proposal

What Required Human Judgment

  • Choosing the final redesign direction
  • Deciding what is realistic for Harlem
  • Evaluating safety and accessibility
  • Determining how landmarks support businesses

Conclusion

The final proposal turns walking into a visible community activity by connecting movement, AI verification, landmarks, and local rewards in one redesign concept for the plaza.
