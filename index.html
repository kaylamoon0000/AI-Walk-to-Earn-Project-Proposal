About the Project

Project Title: AI Walk-to-Earn Harlem: Incentivizing Pedestrian Activity in the Harlem Plaza.

Project Goal: Design a proposal for a smart plaza experience that motivates people to walk along safe routes, interact with landmark checkpoints, and receive incentives from local businesses.

Essential Question: How can artificial intelligence encourage people to walk more while supporting local businesses in a pedestrian plaza?

The Problem

Many pedestrian plazas struggle with low engagement, uneven business activity, weak incentives for exploration, and safety concerns when spaces feel underused. Without a system that connects walking behavior to visible rewards, the plaza can remain inactive and disconnected from nearby businesses.

This redesign proposal addresses that problem by turning movement into a meaningful activity: users walk, verify progress, scan a landmark code near businesses, and receive rewards tied to real participation.

Site / Context

The Harlem plaza redesign focuses on how people move, where they pause, what feels safe, and how local businesses can benefit from foot traffic. Key site understanding areas include movement patterns, comfort, safety, accessibility, and business activity.

The proposal assumes that landmarks can be placed strategically near stores, food spots, and gathering points so users are guided through the plaza instead of passing through it without engaging.

Design Options

Before selecting the final direction, the redesign considers multiple ways AI and pedestrian engagement could work together in the plaza.

Option 1: Route Rewards

Users earn rewards only by walking verified safe routes. This improves movement but has limited proof of business interaction.

Option 2: Business Check-In Model

Users receive rewards when arriving near stores. This supports businesses but depends too heavily on location data alone.

Option 3: Landmark + QR Confirmation

Users walk, reach a public landmark near a business, and scan a code for confirmation. This creates stronger proof of real visits and a more engaging plaza journey.

Final Redesign Proposal

The selected proposal combines AI walking detection, GPS route verification, and landmark QR scanning to create a realistic walk-to-earn experience in Harlem.

In the final proposal, landmarks are placed strategically near local businesses and important decision points in the plaza. Pedestrians follow suggested routes, scan a QR code at each landmark, and receive verified progress toward coupons, points, or small food rewards.

This approach is justified because it improves verification, increases interaction with the environment, creates stronger support for local businesses, and gives the plaza a more active identity through visible checkpoints and guided movement.

Reflection / Conclusion

AI helped generate ideas, structure the proposal, imagine redesign visuals, and organize how walking, landmarks, and rewards could work together. Human judgment was still necessary to decide what was realistic for Harlem, what felt safe and accessible, where landmarks should be placed, and which final proposal best served community needs.

What AI Helped With

  • Generating concept directions
  • Suggesting plaza redesign visuals
  • Structuring route and reward ideas
  • Supporting presentation language

What Required Human Judgment

  • Choosing the final redesign
  • Considering safety and accessibility
  • Matching ideas to local business needs
  • Evaluating what is realistic and useful

Conclusion

The proposal shows that a pedestrian plaza can become more engaging when walking is connected to landmarks, rewards, and thoughtful AI support instead of passive movement alone.
