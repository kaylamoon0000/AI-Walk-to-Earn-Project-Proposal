About the Project

Project Title: AI Walk-to-Earn Harlem: Incentivizing Pedestrian Activity in the Harlem Plaza.

Project Goal: Create a redesign proposal for a pedestrian plaza experience where users walk along safe routes, verify visits at landmarks, and receive business-supported rewards.

Essential Question: How can artificial intelligence encourage people to walk more while supporting local businesses in a pedestrian plaza?

The Problem

Many pedestrian plazas feel underused because people move through them quickly, businesses do not benefit enough from foot traffic, and there are few incentives for visitors to explore the space in a safe and intentional way.

This redesign tries to solve that real-world problem by turning walking into an active experience with route guidance, landmark confirmation, and visible rewards that connect movement to community and business activity.

Site / Context

The proposal studies how people move through the Harlem plaza, where they feel comfortable stopping, which routes feel safest, how accessible the pathways are, and where nearby businesses would benefit most from more pedestrian engagement.

Landmarks are imagined as strategic checkpoints near businesses and gathering areas so that the redesign supports movement, comfort, safety, accessibility, use patterns, and business activity all at once.

Design Options

Several directions were considered before selecting the final redesign proposal.

Option 1: Route Reward Model

Users complete verified walking routes and earn rewards. This improves movement but offers less confirmation of direct interaction with landmarks or businesses.

Option 2: Business Check-In Model

Users receive rewards by reaching store areas. This supports local businesses but depends too much on location alone for confirmation.

Option 3: Landmark QR Proposal

Users walk a route, stop at a landmark near a business, and scan a QR code. This creates stronger visit verification and a more interactive plaza experience.

Final Proposal

The selected redesign combines AI walking detection, route verification, and landmark QR scanning to create a more engaging and community-supporting pedestrian plaza.

In the final proposal, pedestrians follow safe routes, reach landmarks positioned near local businesses, and scan a code to confirm their visit. The scan unlocks points, discounts, coupons, or small food rewards.

This redesign is justified because it increases verification, encourages exploration, makes business participation more direct, and gives the plaza a stronger identity through visible checkpoints and guided movement.

Reflection / Conclusion

This proposal also explains what AI contributed to the redesign process and what still required human judgment.

What AI Helped With

  • Generating design directions
  • Imagining proposal visuals
  • Structuring route and reward ideas
  • Organizing the presentation of the concept

What Required Human Judgment

  • Choosing the final redesign
  • Evaluating realism for Harlem
  • Deciding safe and accessible landmark placement
  • Balancing community needs with business goals

Conclusion

The final redesign proposal shows how AI can support a more active plaza, but the final decisions still depend on human understanding of public space, local context, and community benefit.
