AI Walk-to-Earn: Harlem

A smart urban prototype incentivizing health, local commerce, and community safety through artificial intelligence.

  • About the Project

Project Overview

Redefining urban movement in the heart of Harlem.

The AI Walk-to-Earn Harlem project is an innovative urban design and public health initiative. Our goal is to design a prototype system that encourages pedestrians to explore the Harlem Plaza by rewarding them for their activity. By combining urban design, AI, and community economics, we transform a simple walk into a socially engaging and economically beneficial experience.

The Essential Question: How can artificial intelligence encourage people to walk more while supporting local businesses in a pedestrian plaza?

The Problem

Modern pedestrian plazas often suffer from "Empty Space Syndrome." Despite the infrastructure, these areas can lack active engagement, leading to safety concerns and a disconnect between foot traffic and local merchant success. Without a structured incentive, visitors often take the shortest path possible, leaving local businesses on the periphery unnoticed and under-supported.

Site & Context

Our site analysis of Harlem Plaza identified critical gaps in use patterns. While the main thoroughfares are busy, the secondary scenic routes—where many small businesses are located—remain underused. We are introducing Strategic Landmarks: physical points of interest placed near these local businesses. These landmarks serve as "checkpoints" where users must physically scan a code, ensuring they don't just walk past, but actually engage with the storefronts.

AI Design Components

The technical architecture behind the reward system.

Activity Recognition

Using accelerometer and motion data, our AI differentiates between actual walking and "cheating" (like driving or biking) to ensure the integrity of the health data.

Route Verification

The system uses high-precision GPS to confirm that pedestrians are staying within designated safe zones and following the recommended "high-impact" business routes.

Landmark Interaction

Strategic physical landmarks require a manual scan. This adds a layer of physical proof-of-presence that GPS alone cannot provide, funneling users directly to shop entrances.

Reward Optimization

A machine learning model analyzes user preferences to offer personalized rewards—such as free coffee, loyalty points, or store discounts—that are most likely to convert a walker into a customer.

Final Redesign Proposal

A Unified Ecosystem for Harlem.

The final proposal introduces a three-tiered app interface. The User Interface allows residents to start walks, discover new landmarks, and track their progress toward rewards. The Business Dashboard allows local owners to upload real-time promotions to lure in nearby walkers. Finally, the City Manager View provides heatmaps of pedestrian density, allowing for better data-driven decisions on plaza maintenance and safety patrols.

By gamifying the urban experience, we create "eyes on the street," which naturally increases the perceived and actual safety of the Harlem Plaza while providing a measurable economic boost to the community.

Reflection & Conclusion

Human Reflection: Throughout the design process, AI served as a powerful tool for simulating pedestrian movement and drafting complex sensor-logic code. However, human judgment was essential in determining the cultural placement of landmarks. AI can find the most efficient path, but only humans can identify which areas are meaningful to the Harlem community.

Our team believes this prototype proves that technology, when applied with community-first goals, can bridge the gap between digital convenience and physical neighborhood vitality.
